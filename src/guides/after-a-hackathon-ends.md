The hours after a hackathon are easy to waste. Everyone is tired, the deadline has passed and it is tempting to close the laptop. A little effort in the next few days decides whether the weekend becomes a portfolio piece, a prize you actually receive, a useful lesson or nothing at all.

## In the first day

- **Thank your team.** Then agree, in writing, what happens to the project next. Even "we archive it" is a decision.
- **Save everything.** Download the demo video, screenshots, slides and photos. Event pages and chat servers do not always stay up.
- **Check the submission one more time.** Make sure the project page shows every teammate and that the links and video still work.
- **Collect contacts.** Mentors, judges and other teams you want to stay in touch with.

## While you wait for results

Results can come at a closing ceremony the same day or weeks later for large online events. The organiser is responsible for announcing winners within the timeframe set in the rules, so check there and in the event's announcement channels.

On hackathon.so, ended event pages show winners once they are published on Devpost or Devfolio, copied exactly as the platform states them. Until then, the page says the winners have not been published yet.

## If you won

- **Read the prize rules again.** Claiming a prize often involves forms, tax details, proof of eligibility or a deadline to respond.
- **Reply quickly** to the organisers and any sponsors who contact you.
- **Agree how to split the prize** within the team, ideally before the money arrives.
- **Say thank you publicly.** Mention the organisers, sponsors and mentors who helped.

## If you did not win

Most teams do not win, including many very good ones. Turn it into something useful:

- **Ask for feedback.** Many judges are happy to share a sentence or two if you ask politely.
- **Study the winners.** Look at the winning projects on the event's gallery or results page. What did they show in the first thirty seconds? How did they describe the problem?
- **Write a short retrospective.** Three questions are enough: what went well, what went badly, what will we do differently next time?

## Decide the project's future

Choose one, deliberately:

- **Keep building.** Set a small next milestone and a date. Projects that continue usually do so because someone owns them.
- **Open source it.** Add a licence, a clear README and honest notes on what works.
- **Archive it.** Make sure the repository explains what it was, mark it as unmaintained and move on with a clear conscience.

Whatever you choose, update the README so anyone who finds it later understands the project's state.

## Make the work visible

A hackathon project is evidence of what you can do under real constraints. Make it easy to see:

- Write a short post about what you built and what you learned.
- Add it to your portfolio or CV with a link to the demo video.
- Keep your platform profile up to date. On Devfolio, for example, submitted projects appear on your profile automatically.
- Credit your teammates by name.

## Look after yourself

Hackathons are tiring. Sleep, eat properly and take a break before jumping into the next one. Burnout makes the next event worse, not better.
