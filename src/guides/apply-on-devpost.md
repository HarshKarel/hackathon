Devpost hosts a large share of the world's hackathons, from weekend student events to month-long online challenges run by companies. Applying is not hard, but it has more steps than people expect, and missing one near the deadline is a common way to lose a finished project.

This guide follows the process as Devpost's own Help Center describes it. Each hackathon can add its own rules on top, so always read the event's rules page as well.

## Before you start

- **Read the rules.** The event's rules page sets eligibility, judging criteria, required technologies and limits such as maximum video length.
- **Check the deadline in your time zone.** Devpost shows a submission deadline. On hackathon.so, each event page converts it to your local time.
- **Agree on your team.** Decide who will create the project, since that person adds everyone else.

## Step 1: Create a Devpost account

Sign up at devpost.com. Note that creating an account does not register you for any hackathon. You still need to join each event separately.

## Step 2: Join the hackathon

Open the hackathon's page and click **Join hackathon**. This button appears while the submission period is open. Clicking it completes your registration.

You can unregister at any time until you begin a draft submission, using the **Unregister** link in the right sidebar of the hackathon page.

## Step 3: Start your project

Once registered, the hackathon's overview page offers two options in the sidebar:

- **Start project** creates a new, untitled project for this hackathon.
- **Import from portfolio** turns a project you already have on Devpost into a draft submission. If you use it, update the project to meet this hackathon's rules.

You can also get a head start on the form from the **My projects** tab.

## Step 4: Add your team

In the **Manage team** step, add teammates by entering their email addresses or by sharing an invitation link. Add everyone before you submit, so the whole team is credited.

## Step 5: Project overview

Enter the required **project name** and **tagline**. You can also add a thumbnail for the project gallery: JPG, PNG or GIF, up to 5 MB, with a 3:2 ratio recommended.

## Step 6: Project details

This is the heart of the submission:

- **Your story.** A written description of the project. It supports Markdown.
- **Built with.** Tags for the languages, frameworks, APIs and tools you used (up to 25).
- **Try it out links.** A live site, an app store link or a repository.
- **Image gallery.** Screenshots of the product.
- **Video demo link.** Many hackathons require one. It must be hosted publicly on YouTube or Vimeo so it embeds for judges. Check the hackathon's rules for any maximum length.

Write the first paragraph of your story for a judge reading quickly: who it is for, what it does and what is impressive about it.

## Step 7: Additional details

Each hackathon sets its own questions here. Examples include a code repository URL, a category or track, your country of residence or a file upload. File uploads have a size limit (35 MB, in zip, pdf, Word or apk format, according to Devpost's Help Center). Answer every required question.

## Step 8: Submit

Agree to the hackathon's terms and conditions, then click **Submit project**. If any required step is incomplete, an error message points you to it.

When the submission goes through, a green notification appears at the top of the page, and on your **My projects** page the project is tagged **Submitted**.

## Step 9: Proofread before the deadline

You can go back and edit your project at any point before the submission deadline. Use **View** to check how it looks to judges:

- Does the video play, and is it public?
- Do the links work in a private browser window?
- Is every teammate listed?
- Did you replace any placeholder text or test video?

## Tips that save submissions

- **Submit a complete draft early.** Because you can edit until the deadline, there is no reason to wait for the final version.
- **Submit well ahead on review events.** Some hackathons, marked with a review icon on the overview page, check baseline eligibility for submissions made at least one week before the deadline, which gives you time to fix problems.
- **Upload the video early.** Processing on the video host can take time.
- **Read the rules once more.** Required items differ between events.

## Who decides the winners

Most hackathons on Devpost are run independently by their organisers, who review submissions and choose winners according to their rules. When winners are published on Devpost, they appear on the matching event page on hackathon.so. See [how judging usually works](/guides/how-judging-usually-works/).
