There are hundreds of hackathons open at any moment, and most of them are a poor fit for any one person. Picking well matters more than people expect: the right event gives you a team, a deadline that forces you to finish, and judges who care about the kind of work you do. The wrong one costs a weekend and leaves you with a half-built project nobody looks at.

This guide walks through the questions worth answering before you click register.

## Start with what you want out of it

Be honest about why you are going. The answer changes which event is right.

- **To learn something new.** Look for beginner-friendly events, workshops in the schedule and mentors on site. Prize size matters less than support.
- **To ship something you can show.** Favour longer events (a full weekend or a multi-week online round) so you have time to finish and polish.
- **To win.** Read the judging criteria and the prize list closely, and pick events where your strengths match what is scored.
- **To meet people or get hired.** In-person events with sponsors who are hiring, or events run by a company you want to work with, are worth more than a bigger prize pool.

Most people want a mix. Rank your top two and use them to break ties.

## Check that you are eligible, first

Eligibility rules rule people out more often than anything else, and they are easy to miss. Before anything else, read who can take part. On hackathon.so, every event page shows eligibility exactly as the source states it, under "Who can take part".

Common limits to look for:

- **Student status.** Many events are for students only, sometimes only for one university or for high school students.
- **Age.** Some require participants to be over the age of majority where they live; others set a minimum age with parental consent below it.
- **Country.** Prize eligibility often excludes some countries or limits entry to residents of one country.
- **Employment.** Some events exclude professionals or employees of the sponsor.

If the rules are unclear, ask the organiser before you build anything. Finding out after the deadline that your team cannot receive a prize is avoidable.

## Read the format and the clock

Three facts decide whether you can actually attend:

1. **Format.** Online, in person or hybrid. In-person events need travel and a place to sleep; online events need a quiet place to work for the whole run.
2. **Length.** A 24-hour event and a six-week online round need very different plans. Check both the start and end dates, not just the deadline.
3. **Time zone.** Online deadlines are set in the organiser's time zone. On every event page here, the deadline is also converted into your own time zone, so you can see when it really closes for you.

Then look for two different deadlines. A **registration deadline** is the last moment to sign up. A **submission deadline** is when your project must be in. Some platforms list only the submission deadline, because you can join until then. Some events also review applications before accepting you, which adds days to the process.

## Look at the theme and the judging criteria

Open-ended events let you build anything. Themed events, and events with tracks, reward work in a specific area such as climate, health or developer tools. Sponsor prizes often require using a particular API or product.

Read the judging criteria on the event page. If they emphasise impact and presentation and you love deep technical work, you can still enter, but plan time for the pitch. If no criteria are published, that tells you something about how organised the event is likely to be.

## Read the prize list, not just the total

A large total can be split across many small track prizes, sponsor prizes and non-cash rewards. Check:

- how many prizes there are and what each is for,
- whether prizes are cash, credits, hardware or opportunities such as interviews,
- what you must do to claim them (forms, tax details, proof of eligibility).

On hackathon.so, prize text is shown as the source states it. Follow the link to the source for the full rules.

## Count the hidden costs

Some events charge a registration fee. In-person events can mean travel, a night away and missed work or classes. Online events still cost a weekend. None of this is a reason to skip an event, but it belongs in the decision.

## A quick checklist

Score each candidate event from 0 to 2 on each line, then compare totals.

| Question | 0 | 2 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Am I clearly eligible? | Unsure | Yes, in writing |
| Can I attend the whole event? | Clashes | Clear calendar |
| Does the theme fit my skills or goals? | Not really | Strongly |
| Are judging criteria published? | No | Yes, and they suit us |
| Do I have, or can I find, a team? | No | Yes |
| Is the deadline realistic for me? | Very tight | Comfortable |

Anything under 6 is probably not your event this time.
