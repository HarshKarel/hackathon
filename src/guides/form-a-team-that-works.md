Most hackathon projects that fall apart do so because of the team, not the code. The good news is that the causes are predictable: unclear roles, different goals and no agreed way to decide things. A short conversation at the start fixes most of them.

## Check the rules first

Every event sets its own team rules. On hackathon.so, the team size is shown on the event page when the source states it. Look for:

- **Minimum and maximum size.** Limits of three or four people are common, but read the actual rule.
- **Whether solo entries are allowed.** Some platforms require a team record even if you work alone.
- **Who must register.** Usually every member registers individually, then joins the team.

Platforms handle teams differently. On Devpost, teammates are added to the project submission by email or an invitation link. On Devfolio, one person creates the team and shares an invite code, and in events with application review every member must also be accepted and RSVP. The step-by-step guides for [Devpost](/guides/apply-on-devpost/) and [Devfolio](/guides/apply-on-devfolio/) cover the details.

## Aim for coverage, not stars

A strong team covers the work a hackathon actually needs:

- **Building the core.** One or two people who can write the main logic and connect it to data or APIs.
- **The front end and experience.** Someone who makes the demo usable and clear.
- **Product and story.** Someone who keeps the scope honest, writes the submission and leads the pitch.

People can hold more than one role. What matters is that each part has an owner. Four excellent back-end developers can still lose to three people who split the work well.

## Where to find teammates

- People you already work or study with, if your goals match.
- The event's own channels: many events run a chat server, a team-forming session or a "looking for team" thread.
- Local tech communities and student clubs before in-person events.

When you meet someone new, ask what they want from the event and what they enjoy building. Those two answers predict how the weekend will go better than a list of skills.

## Agree on five things before you start

Spend fifteen minutes on these at the start. Write the answers down where everyone can see them.

1. **The goal.** Are you trying to win, to learn or to ship something you will keep? A team split between "win" and "learn" will argue all weekend.
2. **Working hours.** Who is sleeping when, and when is everyone online together?
3. **Tools.** One repository, one task list, one chat thread. Decide the stack now.
4. **Who decides.** When the team disagrees, who makes the final call on product questions? On technical questions?
5. **The demo owner.** One person is responsible for the demo working at the end.

## Keep communication light and regular

- **Short check-ins every few hours.** What did you finish, what is next, what is blocking you?
- **A visible task list.** Sticky notes, a shared board or a checklist in the repository.
- **Merge often.** Integration problems found at 3 a.m. on the last night are the worst kind.

## Handle disagreements quickly

Disagreement is normal under time pressure. Keep it small:

- Timebox the discussion to a few minutes.
- If both options are reasonable, let the person who will build it choose.
- Prefer the option that gets you to a working demo sooner.
- Revisit the decision only if new information appears.

## Remote and hybrid teams

Online events add time zones. Agree on overlap hours when everyone is available and use them for decisions. Outside those hours, leave written updates so nobody wakes up blocked. Record quick screen captures instead of long explanations.

## Look after each other

Make space for newer teammates to contribute and ask questions. Take breaks. Make sure everyone eats and sleeps. A rested team at the final presentation does better than an exhausted one with two extra features.

## If it is not working

If a teammate disappears or the team stops functioning, talk early and adjust scope. Most events allow you to continue with fewer people; check the rules if you need to change team members on the platform before submitting.
